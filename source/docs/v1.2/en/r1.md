## FAQ

1.  How do I enter the R1’s BIOS menu?

    > Upon power-up, press `F2` within 0.5 seconds to enter the BIOS, or `F12` to access the boot menu.

2.  How do I set auto power-on or manual power-on/off?

    > Press `F2` within 0.5 seconds of powering on to enter BIOS. Navigate to `Chipset` > `PCH-IO Configuration` > `State After G3`. Set `S0 State` for auto power-on, or `S5 State` for manual power control.

3.  How do I unlock the thermal throttle limit?

    > Press `F2` at startup to enter BIOS. Go to `Advanced` > `Thermal Configuration` > `CPU Thermal Configuration` > `Tcc Activation Offset`. Adjust the default value of 30 (which sets the throttle at 105°C - 30 = 75°C). Setting it to 0 unlocks the limit at 105°C, but we recommend setting it to 15 (105°C - 15 = 90°C). Be aware, increasing this limit may cause higher overall system temperatures.

4.  Does the R1 support both NVMe and SATA storage?

    > Currently, the R1 only supports one protocol at a time, defaulting to NVMe. If you prefer the lower-heat SATA protocol, you’ll need to flash a SATA-specific BIOS. Similarly, to switch back to NVMe, you’ll need to reflash the NVMe BIOS. BIOS flashing is a high-risk operation and should be done with caution.

5.  How do I flash the BIOS on the R1?

    > Follow the instructions outlined in the \[BIOS Upgrade Guide\].

6.  Does the R1 support legacy BIOS booting?

    > No, all Intel processors after the 10th generation only support UEFI booting. Legacy BIOS is not supported. However, you can still use legacy systems like Panabit by leveraging virtualization software such as PVE or ESXi.

7.  Can the R1 boot from a TF card?

    > Yes. Linux and its branches (OpenWRT, pfSense, OPNsense) can boot directly from the TF card. For Windows, use the [Rufus tool](https://rufus.ie/downloads/) to create a `WinToGo` bootable drive.

8.  What power specifications does the R1 support?

    > The R1 has a Type-C port with PD markings and supports both DC 12/20V and PD protocols. While it prefers to negotiate 20V first, it can fall back to 12V. Due to this dual-power protocol, it does not use a traditional DC barrel jack, but all orders come with a DC-to-Type-C adapter cable for compatibility with existing DC 12V adapters.
