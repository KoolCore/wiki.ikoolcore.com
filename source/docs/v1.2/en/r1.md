# R1 Documentation

## Hardware Design

![R1 hardware design](https://s2.loli.net/2023/03/18/8kR6c5wLIAbF2ox.jpg)

> Custom design from the ground up. Due to business collaborations, some consumers might encounter rebranded versions of this product _(non-official iKoolCore products, not covered under our warranty)_.

- **CPU:** Celeron N5105, Pentium Silver N6005
- **Memory:** LPDDR4 dual-channel, 8GB single-chip (**`16GB uses two chips for true dual-channel`**)
- **Networking:** 4 x Intel i226-V
- **Storage:** 1 x **M.2 2242 NVMe (PCIe 3.0 x2 speed)**
- **Display:** 1 x HDMI 2.0, supports 4K 60Hz output
- **USB:** 2 x USB 3.1 Gen 2; 1 x USB-C 3.1 Gen 2
- **TF Card Reader:** Supports booting **(tested with Linux and its branches—OpenWRT, pfSense, and OPNsense; Windows booting requires `WinToGo`)**
- **Cooling:** Active cooling **(since 2023, all units feature upgraded copper pipe heatsinks and silent fans)**
- **Power:** Includes a 12V 2.5A 30W DC adapter **(DC barrel jack to USB-C adapter cable included)**
- **Dimensions:** 7.5 x 7.5 x 4.8 cm **(compact in size, limitless fun)**

> **Note:** The custom 12V DC adapter is designed specifically for the R1. Do not use it with other devices, as this may cause damage.

## FAQ

### How do I enter the R1 BIOS menu?

Upon power-up, press `F2` within 0.5 seconds to enter BIOS, or `F12` to access the boot menu.

### How do I set auto power-on or manual power-on/off?

Press `F2` within 0.5 seconds of powering on to enter BIOS. Navigate to `Chipset` → `PCH-IO Configuration` → `State After G3`. Set `S0 State` for auto power-on, or `S5 State` for manual power control.

### How do I unlock the thermal throttle limit?

Press `F2` at startup to enter BIOS. Go to `Advanced` → `Thermal Configuration` → `CPU Thermal Configuration` → `Tcc Activation Offset`. Adjust the default value of 30, which sets the throttle at 105°C − 30 = 75°C. Setting it to 0 unlocks the limit at 105°C, but setting it to 15 is recommended, which gives 105°C − 15 = 90°C. Increasing this limit may cause higher overall system temperatures.

### Does the R1 support both NVMe and SATA storage?

The R1 supports one protocol at a time and defaults to NVMe. To use the lower-heat SATA protocol, flash a SATA-specific BIOS. To switch back to NVMe, reflash the NVMe BIOS. BIOS flashing is a high-risk operation and should be performed with caution.

### How do I flash the BIOS on the R1?

Follow the instructions in the BIOS Upgrade Guide.

### Does the R1 support legacy BIOS booting?

No. Intel processors after the 10th generation only support UEFI booting. Legacy BIOS is not supported. You can still use legacy systems such as Panabit through virtualization software such as PVE or ESXi.

### Can the R1 boot from a TF card?

Yes. Linux and its branches, including OpenWRT, pfSense, and OPNsense, can boot directly from a TF card. For Windows, use [Rufus](https://rufus.ie/downloads/) to create a `WinToGo` bootable drive.

### What power specifications does the R1 support?

The R1 has a Type-C port with PD markings and supports both DC 12/20V and PD protocols. It prefers to negotiate 20V first and can fall back to 12V. Because of this dual-power protocol, it does not use a traditional DC barrel jack. All orders include a DC-to-Type-C adapter cable for compatibility with existing DC 12V adapters.
